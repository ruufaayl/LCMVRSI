\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Working draft.} Subquadratic sequence models replace the transformer's growing
key--value cache with a fixed-size recurrent state, and pay for it on associative recall: a
known information-theoretic bound (BASED, Thm.~3.1) shows any causal recurrent model needs
$\Omega(n)$ bits of state to solve multi-query associative recall (MQAR) in the worst case. We do
not reclaim that bound. Instead we observe it is worst-case over near-uniform inputs, whereas
structured streams realize far lower key$\rightarrow$value entropy $H \ll n$. We state two
hypotheses: an entropy-parameterized refinement of the lower bound,
$\Omega((1-H_b(\varepsilon))H)$ bits, that recovers $\Omega(n)$ when $H = \Theta(n)$
\textbf{[CONJECTURE]}, flagged as a possible corollary of existing information-cost arguments;
and a surprise/entropy-gated sparse external memory whose state grows toward $\Theta(H)$,
intended to push the empirical recall--memory Pareto frontier on structured inputs while matching
baselines in the uniform worst case \textbf{[CONJECTURE]}. Every claim is tagged \textbf{[PROVEN]}
/ \textbf{[EMPIRICAL]} / \textbf{[CONJECTURE]}; the empirical Pareto study is the primary
deliverable, with results---including negative ones---populated in later milestones.
\end{abstract}
