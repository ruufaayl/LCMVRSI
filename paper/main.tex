\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Working draft.} Subquadratic sequence models replace the transformer's growing
key--value cache with a fixed-size recurrent state, and pay for it on associative recall: a
known information-theoretic bound (BASED, Thm.~3.1) shows any causal recurrent model needs
$\Omega(n)$ bits of state to solve multi-query associative recall (MQAR) in the worst case. We do
not reclaim that bound. Instead we observe it is worst-case over near-uniform inputs, whereas
structured streams realize far lower key$\rightarrow$value entropy $H \ll n$. We make this
precise: under the standard recurrent-bottleneck assumption we prove an \emph{entropy floor}
$S \ge H(M) - D\,[H_b(\varepsilon) + \varepsilon \log_2(|U|-1)]$ on the state
(Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}, via data processing and Fano), which recovers the $\Omega(n)$ bound
when the realized key$\rightarrow$value entropy $H(M) = \Theta(n)$ and is far smaller when
$H(M) \ll n$ \textbf{[PROVEN]}; the technique is standard and the contribution is the entropy
\emph{reparameterization}. Building on it, we propose a surprise/entropy-gated sparse external
memory whose state grows toward $\Theta(H)$, intended to push the empirical recall--memory Pareto
frontier on structured inputs while matching baselines in the uniform worst case
\textbf{[CONJECTURE]}; a lower bound says nothing about achievability, which we test empirically. Every claim is tagged \textbf{[PROVEN]}
/ \textbf{[EMPIRICAL]} / \textbf{[CONJECTURE]}; the empirical Pareto study is the primary
deliverable, with results---including negative ones---populated in later milestones.
\end{abstract}
